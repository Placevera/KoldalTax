IRS CP2000: Proposed changes to your tax return

High: respond by the deadline For: Individuals

What this notice means

The income or payments reported to the IRS by employers, banks, brokers and others don't match what's on your return. The IRS is proposing changes, which usually means more tax, interest and possibly a penalty. It is not a bill and not a formal audit yet.

Common reasons you got it

  • A missed 1099 (interest, dividends, freelance, stock sales)
  • Stock sales reported without cost basis
  • Retirement distributions or unemployment left off the return

What to do

  • Compare every item on the notice to your records and your return
  • If you agree, sign the response form and pay or set up a payment plan
  • If you disagree in whole or part, send the response form with a signed explanation and documents
  • Don't file an amended return for these changes; respond to the notice instead

Deadline

Respond by the date on the notice, usually 30 days. If you don't, the IRS sends a Statutory Notice of Deficiency and assesses the tax.
